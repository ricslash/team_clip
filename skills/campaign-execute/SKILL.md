# Campaign Execute Skill

## When to Use

The Content & Growth Lead invokes this skill when executing marketing campaigns assigned by the CMO. It covers the full content creation and distribution workflow from draft through publication.

## Content Creation Patterns

### Blog Posts

**Structure:**
```markdown
---
title: "{Post Title — include primary keyword}"
meta_title: "{50-60 chars, keyword near front}"
meta_description: "{150-160 chars, compelling, includes keyword}"
slug: "{url-friendly-slug-with-keyword}"
author: "{author name}"
date: "{YYYY-MM-DD}"
category: "{primary category}"
tags: ["{tag1}", "{tag2}", "{tag3}"]
---

# {H1 — one per post, matches title or is a variation}

{Opening hook: 1-2 sentences that frame the problem or promise. No throat-clearing.}

## {H2 — Section 1: Problem/Context}
{2-4 paragraphs establishing the pain point with specific examples}

## {H2 — Section 2: Solution/Approach}
{The core content — actionable, specific, evidence-based}

### {H3 — Subsection if needed}
{Break long sections into scannable chunks}

## {H2 — Section 3: Implementation/How-To}
{Practical steps, code examples, screenshots as needed}

## {H2 — Section 4: Results/Evidence}
{Data, case studies, before/after, social proof}

## {H2 — Conclusion/CTA}
{Summarize key takeaway in 2-3 sentences. Clear CTA.}
```

**Requirements:**
- Minimum 1500 words for SEO-targeted posts
- Include at least 2 internal links to other product pages or blog posts
- Include at least 1 external link to a credible source
- Use short paragraphs (3-4 sentences max)
- Include a visual break (image, code block, table, or callout) every 300-400 words
- Write for a 9th-grade reading level — clear, not dumbed down

### Social Media Posts

**Twitter/X:**
```
{Hook — first line must stop the scroll, under 100 chars}

{Body — the insight, tip, or story. 1-3 short lines.}

{CTA or link}

{Hashtags — 1-3 relevant, no spam}
```
- Character limit: 280 (leave room for engagement)
- Thread format for longer content: first tweet is the hook, each subsequent tweet is one complete thought
- No emoji spam — 0-2 emojis max, only if they add meaning

**LinkedIn:**
```
{Hook line — pattern interrupt or contrarian take}

{Blank line}

{Story or insight — 3-5 short paragraphs}
{Use line breaks generously for mobile readability}

{Blank line}

{CTA — question to drive comments, or link}

{Blank line}

{Hashtags — 3-5 relevant}
```
- Optimal length: 150-300 words
- Write in first person — LinkedIn rewards personal voice
- No corporate jargon

**Reddit:**
```
Title: {Descriptive, not clickbait. Match subreddit norms.}

Body:
{Context — why you're posting, establish credibility without self-promotion}

{Value — the actual insight, tutorial, or discussion point}

{Genuine question or discussion prompt — Reddit rewards engagement}

{Disclosure if linking to own product: "Disclosure: I built {product}"}
```
- Read and match the subreddit's tone before posting
- Provide value first — 80% value, 20% subtle product mention
- Never post the same content to multiple subreddits without adaptation
- Respond to every comment for the first 24 hours

### Email Sequences

**Welcome/Onboarding Sequence:**

Email 1 — Welcome (send immediately):
```
Subject: {Welcome + immediate value promise}
Subject B: {Alternative for A/B test}
Preview text: {35-90 chars, complements subject, does not repeat it}

Body:
- Greeting (first name if available)
- What they signed up for (1 sentence)
- Single most important next step (with button/link)
- What to expect from emails (frequency, content type)
- Sign-off with real name
```

Email 2 — Quick Win (send day 2):
```
Subject: {Specific benefit they can achieve today}
Preview text: {Teaser of the quick win}

Body:
- Reference their signup (continuity)
- One specific tip or workflow they can do in 5 minutes
- Screenshot or GIF showing the result
- CTA: "Try it now" button
```

Email 3 — Social Proof (send day 4):
```
Subject: {How {persona} uses {product} to {outcome}}
Preview text: {Brief teaser}

Body:
- Mini case study or user quote
- The specific workflow or feature that drove the result
- CTA: "See how it works" or "Start your {workflow}"
```

Email 4 — Feature Depth (send day 7):
```
Subject: {Did you know? Underused feature that solves {pain point}}
Preview text: {Teaser}

Body:
- Name a common pain point
- Show how the feature solves it (step by step)
- CTA: "Try {feature}"
```

Email 5 — Conversion (send day 10):
```
Subject: {Your free trial ends in X days / Upgrade prompt}
Preview text: {Value recap}

Body:
- Recap the value they've gotten (if tracking: reference their usage)
- What they'll lose without upgrading (loss aversion, specific features)
- What they'll gain (specific premium features)
- Pricing context (value framing, not just price)
- CTA: "Upgrade now" button
- Fallback: "Not ready? Reply and tell me what's holding you back"
```

**Requirements for all emails:**
- CAN-SPAM compliance: unsubscribe link, physical mailing address, honest subject line
- Mobile-first design: single column, large tap targets, short lines
- Plain text fallback for every HTML email
- Preview text is distinct from subject line — they work together, not repeat

### Landing Pages

**Structure:**
```
HERO SECTION
- Headline: {Primary value proposition — what they get, not what it is}
- Subheadline: {How it works or who it's for — one sentence}
- CTA button: {Action-oriented: "Start Free", "Try It Now", not "Learn More"}
- Social proof snippet: {"Used by X teams" or "Rated 4.8/5 on G2"}

PROBLEM SECTION
- {Name 3 specific pain points the target audience has}
- {Use their language — pull from user feedback research}

SOLUTION SECTION
- {3-4 feature highlights with benefit-oriented headers}
- {Each feature: icon/image + benefit headline + 1-2 sentence description}
- {Focus on outcomes, not features: "Save 5 hours/week" not "Automated workflows"}

SOCIAL PROOF SECTION
- {User testimonials with name, role, company}
- {Logos of notable customers if available}
- {Specific metrics: "Reduced X by Y%" not "Customers love us"}

PRICING SECTION (if applicable)
- {Tier cards with clear differentiation}
- {Highlight recommended tier}
- {Annual/monthly toggle}
- {CTA on each tier}

FAQ SECTION
- {5-8 common objections framed as questions}
- {Direct, honest answers — no evasion}

FINAL CTA
- {Repeat primary CTA}
- {Risk reversal: "Free trial, no credit card", "Cancel anytime"}
```

## SEO Optimization Checklist

Apply to all blog posts and landing pages:

- [ ] **Primary keyword** identified and appears in: title, H1, first 100 words, meta title, meta description, URL slug
- [ ] **Secondary keywords** (2-3) appear naturally in H2s and body text
- [ ] **Meta title** is 50-60 characters, keyword near the front
- [ ] **Meta description** is 150-160 characters, includes keyword, has a compelling reason to click
- [ ] **URL slug** is short, includes primary keyword, uses hyphens
- [ ] **H1** is unique per page, contains primary keyword
- [ ] **H2/H3 structure** is logical and uses secondary keywords where natural
- [ ] **Internal links** (2+ per post) to relevant product pages or other blog posts
- [ ] **External links** (1+ per post) to credible, authoritative sources
- [ ] **Image alt text** is descriptive, includes keyword where relevant (not stuffed)
- [ ] **Page speed** — images compressed, no render-blocking scripts above fold
- [ ] **Mobile responsive** — tested on mobile viewport
- [ ] **Schema markup** — Article schema for blog posts, Product schema for landing pages (if supported by platform)

## Platform-Specific Formatting

| Platform | Max Length | Tone | Visual | Link Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Twitter/X | 280 chars | Punchy, conversational | 1 image or GIF | Links in last tweet of thread |
| LinkedIn | 3000 chars | Professional but personal | Native images perform best | Links in first comment (algorithm penalty for links in post) |
| Reddit | No limit | Match subreddit norms | Inline images if allowed | Self-post preferred over link posts |
| Blog | 1500+ words | Expert, accessible | Break every 300-400 words | Internal + external linking |
| Email | Under 200 words per email | Direct, personal | Minimal images, one CTA | Single primary CTA button |
| Landing Page | N/A | Benefit-oriented, concise | Heavy visual | Every section has a CTA |

## Quality Checklist Before Publishing

Every piece of public-facing content must pass these checks before publication:

### Content Quality
- [ ] Headline is specific, compelling, and accurate (no clickbait)
- [ ] Opening line hooks the reader within 10 words
- [ ] Every paragraph earns its place — cut anything that does not advance the reader's understanding
- [ ] No unsupported claims or unsubstantiated superlatives ("best", "fastest", "#1" require evidence)
- [ ] CTA is clear, specific, and appears at natural decision points
- [ ] Proofread for grammar, spelling, and punctuation (no reliance on spellcheck alone)

### Brand Consistency
- [ ] Tone matches brand voice guidelines
- [ ] Visual elements use approved colors, typography, and spacing
- [ ] Product name is correctly capitalized and formatted everywhere
- [ ] Messaging aligns with approved positioning and messaging hierarchy
- [ ] No contradictions with other live marketing materials

### Compliance
- [ ] No misleading claims or deceptive patterns
- [ ] Required disclaimers present where applicable
- [ ] CAN-SPAM compliant (emails): unsubscribe link, physical address, honest subject
- [ ] Platform-specific advertising policies followed (if paid promotion)
- [ ] GDPR/CCPA compliant if collecting any data

### Brand Review Gate
- [ ] **All public-facing content is blocked on brand review gate** — assigned to Design Lead
- [ ] Content submitted for review with full context (target audience, channel, goal)
- [ ] Design Lead has approved brand consistency, visual elements, and tone
- [ ] If content was revised after initial review, re-submitted for approval

### Technical
- [ ] All links work and point to correct destinations
- [ ] Images load correctly and have alt text
- [ ] Mobile rendering verified
- [ ] Tracking parameters (UTM) added to all links where applicable
- [ ] No broken HTML or formatting artifacts

## Workflow

1. **Receive assignment** — CMO or CoS creates content task with requirements document
2. **Research** — Review the `research` document on the parent milestone (from Market Researcher)
3. **Draft** — Create content following the appropriate pattern above
4. **Self-review** — Run through the quality checklist above
5. **Submit for brand review** — Comment on the issue requesting Design Lead review. CoS auto-creates the brand review gate issue.
6. **Submit for legal review** — If content makes claims about data handling, pricing, or competitive comparisons, request Legal Counsel review.
7. **Revise** — Address review feedback. Re-submit if substantive changes were made.
8. **Publish** — Once all gates pass, publish to the target platform.
9. **Document** — Update the issue with the published URL and any platform-specific IDs (tweet ID, post URL, etc.)
